# RepairDesk manual matching playbook

This is the operating procedure for the enquiry-first pilot. It is intentionally manual: the current dashboard does not create provider assignments, send introductions, or confirm bookings.

## 1. Review a new customer enquiry

1. Open the private admin dashboard and select **Customer enquiries**.
2. Check the device model, issue, area and contact number.
3. Change `new` to `reviewing` only when someone has started reviewing it.
4. Check whether a suitable, currently operating provider has been approved for that exact service and area.
5. Do not promise availability, price, repair time or pickup until the provider confirms it.

## 2. Check provider suitability

Before considering a provider, manually confirm:
- The person is genuinely connected to the repair business and can be contacted.
- The business operates in the stated area.
- The provider actually performs the required repair and has the relevant capability.
- The provider accepts the pilot's no-fee/no-commission terms and understands their responsibilities.
- The provider has agreed how diagnosis, final quote, warranty, device custody, accessories, pickup/return, cancellation and customer complaints are handled.

The dashboard's `verified` status is an internal approval flag, not government verification or a guarantee. Never publish an applicant based only on a form submission or phone-format check.

## 3. Confirm a match

1. Contact the provider using the submitted business contact details. Share only the minimum repair details needed; do not share unnecessary customer personal data.
2. Ask the provider to confirm they can take this specific repair, in the customer's area, within a timeframe they state.
3. If they decline or do not respond, keep the enquiry in `reviewing` and try another eligible provider. Do not mark it matched.
4. Once a provider explicitly agrees, contact the customer and introduce the provider. Make clear that the customer can decide whether to proceed.
5. Mark the enquiry `matched` only after the provider has agreed and the introduction has actually been made.
6. The provider and customer then agree the diagnosis, final quote, repair warranty, device handover and any logistics directly. The customer pays the provider directly.

## 4. Close or stop

- Use `closed` when the enquiry is completed, the customer declines, or no suitable provider is available after reasonable attempts. Record the reason in an approved internal record if one exists; do not put sensitive details in public notes.
- Use `spam` only for clearly abusive or fraudulent submissions.
- Never mark an enquiry `matched` merely because a provider was suggested, contacted, or selected internally.

## 5. Privacy and device safety

- Keep the admin dashboard private. Do not post screenshots containing names or phone numbers.
- Use contact details only to process the relevant enquiry/application.
- Never request device passcodes, OTPs, banking PINs, passwords or account recovery codes.
- Before a device is handed over, the customer and provider should document device condition, accessories, agreed work, price, warranty and handover time.
- Do not promise RepairDesk insurance, repair guarantees, dispute resolution or provider availability unless those services are actually established.

## Current pilot limitations

This is a human-operated enquiry and provider-application workflow, not automated matching or confirmed booking. The admin dashboard does not currently store a provider-to-enquiry assignment, introduction timestamp, quote, appointment or handover record. Maintain any operational follow-up in a secure, access-controlled process until those features are deliberately built.
